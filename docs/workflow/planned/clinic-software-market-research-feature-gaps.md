# Competitive market research — core missing features & subscription-driving differentiators

**Status:** Done. Phases 33–39 shipped. Multi-branch reporting (§4.2 #5) stays deferred per this doc's own recommendation. Archive: [`docs/workflow/done/ph-market-research-backlog.md`](../done/ph-market-research-backlog.md).
**Type:** Market/competitive research pass, not an engineering plan — produces a prioritized feature backlog to fold into `docs/mvp.md` and `docs/phases/` after the open decisions in §5 are resolved with the user.
**Do not implement from this document directly** — this is the saved output of a planning/research pass (`.cursor/rules/plan-mode.mdc`). It identifies gaps; it does not design the implementation. Each accepted item still needs its own spec pass against `docs/mvp.md`'s conventions (data model, RBAC tier, AI tool tier if applicable, doc updates) before it becomes a phase.
**Scope of the ask:** research international and Philippine dental/doctor/clinic systems, identify (a) core features we're missing that we need to close the gap, and (b) nice-to-have features that would make clinics/doctors want to subscribe — for both markets.

## 1. Method and confidence level

This is web research synthesized against `docs/mvp.md` v1.1 (current MVP scope, all 19 phases). Sources are cited inline and listed in §7. Two caveats:

- **International sources are strong and current** (2026 vendor comparison articles, direct vendor comparison pages) — high confidence.
- **Philippine sources are thinner.** There is no single authoritative "PH clinic software market report" — findings come from vendor marketing pages (SeriousMD, Agimat Health, Bizbox, Exist, Synodus's HIS roundup) and a software-directory listing (SoftwareSuggest PH). Treat feature _claims_ from vendor sites as marketing, not verified fact, and treat regulatory specifics (PhilHealth HITP certification, DOH EMR accreditation, BIR CAS/EIS) as **directionally correct but needing legal/compliance verification before being scheduled into a phase** — this doc flags where that verification step is required rather than treating vendor-page claims as settled fact.

## 2. International landscape — what "table stakes" and "differentiator" look like today

### 2.1 General practice EHR/PM (Tebra, athenahealth, DrChrono, SimplePractice, Practice Fusion, eClinicalWorks)

Feature categories every serious comparison evaluates ([Tebra vs Athenahealth vs SimplePractice](https://www.tebra.com/compare-tebra-vs-athenahealth-vs-simplepractice), [Commure best-EHR guide](https://www.commure.com/blog-scribe/best-ehr-for-solo-practice)):

- **EHR/compliance:** certification status (US: ONC), security frameworks (HIPAA/HITRUST/SOC 2/PCI-DSS), specialty templates, telehealth built in, ePrescribing, lab ordering.
- **AI:** ambient clinical documentation is now the single most-marketed differentiator across the entire category — "clinical documentation is the top driver of provider burnout" is the standard sales pitch. athenahealth's `athenaAmbient` and Tebra's AI charting are positioned as the reason to switch, not an add-on.
- **Revenue cycle management (RCM):** claims workflow visibility, real-time eligibility verification, denial prevention _before_ submission (not just after-the-fact rejection handling), credit-card processing built in.
- **Patient experience / practice growth (this is the part our MVP doesn't touch at all):** web profile/SEO tools, **online reputation management** (review requests, AI-drafted review responses), online self-scheduling, two-way text messaging, patient portal.
- **Pricing structure itself is a competitive lever** — athenahealth ties price to collections (% of revenue), most others charge flat per-provider ($300–500/mo mid-market band for Tebra). This is a go-to-market decision, not a feature, but it shapes which segment a vendor wins.

### 2.2 Dental-specific PM (Dentrix, Open Dental, Curve Dental, Denticon)

Dental is architecturally different from general medical EMR in one specific way our MVP currently treats as "just a SOAP template" ([First Stop Dental comparison](https://firststopdental.com/blog/dental-practice-management-software-comparison), [Lassie AI dental PMS comparison](https://www.lassie.ai/blog/dental-pms-comparison)):

- **Interactive odontogram + periodontal charting** is a first-class module, not a form field — tooth-by-tooth condition tracking, treatment planning overlaid on the chart, procedure codes tied to specific teeth/surfaces.
- **ePrescribe** is a dedicated module (Dentrix), same pattern as our §6.6 but dental-specific drug sets.
- **Two-way texting + automated reminders** (Dentrix) — same pattern we already have (§6.9), confirms we're not behind here.
- **Cloud-native vs. legacy-adapted-to-cloud** is a real differentiator buyers weigh (Curve Dental markets itself as "built for the browser from day one" vs. Dentrix Ascend being a legacy desktop product ported to cloud) — this is actually a point in our favor since kame-desk is cloud-native from day one.

### 2.3 AI ambient scribes (Abridge, Nuance Dragon Copilot, Suki, Nabla, DeepScribe, Freed)

This is the single hottest category in the entire industry right now ([Vera Health 2026 roundup](https://www.verahealth.ai/blog/best-ai-medical-scribes-ambient-documentation-2026)):

- Standard pipeline: capture audio → transcribe → LLM drafts SOAP note → maps to ICD-10/SNOMED/CPT → writes back into the EHR for physician review. **This is exactly our §8.2 architecture** — confirms our plan is aligned with where the market went, not behind it.
- Pricing tiers observed: free/basic ($0–39/mo), mid-tier (~$99/mo), enterprise ($200–600/mo) — useful signal for what a standalone "AI scribe" add-on could be priced at if we ever separate it from the core subscription.
- The market has stratified into enterprise (deep Epic/Oracle bidirectional write-back) vs. mid-market/specialty (workflow fit, specialty accuracy) vs. solo-clinician tools (Freed). **kame-desk's AI assistant, being built natively into our own EHR rather than bolted onto someone else's, is closer to the enterprise deep-integration pattern than a bolt-on scribe** — worth stating explicitly as a differentiator in our own marketing once shipped.

### 2.4 Patient portal / telehealth / e-prescribing ([Arkenea 2026 patient portal guide](https://arkenea.com/blog/patient-portal-features/), [RXNT e-prescribing roundup](https://www.rxnt.com/the-top-5-e-prescribing-software-solutions-for-healthcare-providers-in-2026/))

- **99% of US hospitals now offer patients electronic record view; 96% allow download; 84% allow third-party transmission.** A full patient portal (not just our booking link) is now assumed table stakes internationally, which sharpens the tension with our own §14 deferral of it — see §5.1 below.
- **Real e-prescribing** (not just a printable PDF) means routing to a pharmacy's queue plus **automatic drug-interaction and insurance-formulary checking**, with delivery status visible to the patient. Our §6.6 explicitly stops at "printable PDF" and defers pharmacy submission — correct call for MVP given PH pharmacy-network integration barely exists (see §3), but worth re-confirming this is still the right MVP line once we look at PH-specific pharmacy chains (Mercury Drug, Watsons) rather than assuming the US e-Rx model transfers.
- **Telehealth is now expected to share one login/workflow with the rest of the EHR** — "scheduling, intake, e-prescribing, and follow-up orders all happen within a single workflow." We've explicitly deferred telemedicine video to Phase 2 (§14) — reasonable, but if we ever build it, the lesson from this research is: don't bolt on a separate video tool, build it into the same visit record.

### 3.2 PH-specific regulatory/compliance surface (needs legal verification before scheduling)

Three separate compliance tracks came up repeatedly across PH vendor sites, and **none of them are currently represented as concrete build items in `docs/mvp.md`** — today they're either absent or reduced to a single open question:

1. **PhilHealth e-claims / HITP accreditation.** Direct electronic claims submission requires either the software itself passing PhilHealth's certification process, or routing through a PhilHealth-**accredited Health Information Technology Provider (HITP)**. Multiple PH competitors (Bizbox, WAH, Agimat) lead their marketing with this accreditation. Today `mvp.md` §6.7 only tracks HMO/insurance claims as "structured notes," and PhilHealth isn't mentioned at all outside the general "Philippines-first" open question (§15.1). **This is a certification/partnership decision, not just a feature** — building our own PhilHealth-certified e-claims pipeline is a multi-month regulatory undertaking disproportionate to an MVP; the more realistic path (mirroring how small US EHRs use a clearinghouse instead of self-certifying to a payer) is likely to **integrate with an existing accredited HITP as a claims-submission partner** rather than certify kame-desk itself. Needs a business decision, not an engineering one, before scheduling.
2. **DOH EMR accreditation.** Cited as a trust signal by Bizbox, Exist, MEDtrix. Not currently addressed anywhere in `mvp.md`. Lower urgency than PhilHealth (doesn't block clinic operations the way claims-submission does) but likely to come up in any serious PH sales conversation with a clinic evaluating vendors side by side.
3. **BIR receipt compliance depth.** `mvp.md` §6.7 currently says "sequential receipt numbering, configurable to match... BIR Official Receipt numbering" — this understates the actual requirement. Legitimate computer-generated receipts/invoices in the Philippines generally require either a BIR **Permit to Use (PTU)** for a computerized/loose-leaf system, or **Computerized Accounting System (CAS)** accreditation, and BIR has been expanding its **Electronic Invoicing System (EIS)** mandate. "Configurable numbering format" alone does not satisfy this — it needs its own compliance workstream (whose responsibility: kame-desk's or each individual clinic's BIR registration? — open question, see §5).

**None of the above were independently re-verified against primary government sources in this pass** (NPC/PhilHealth/BIR official sites) — flagging this explicitly rather than presenting vendor-marketing claims as settled compliance fact. **Recommend a dedicated compliance-verification pass (ideally with PH healthcare/tax counsel) before committing engineering time to any of the three**, since getting the shape of the integration wrong after building it is expensive to unwind.

### 3.3 PH-specific product/UX signals

- **Offline resilience is treated as a headline feature, not an edge case**, by every PH-native competitor (SeriousMD, ClinicEMR). This is a meaningfully different posture from our current architecture (`apps/api` is "always-on," `apps/web` has no offline-first design in `tech-stack.md`). Provincial/rural clinics with unreliable internet are a real, named part of our own target-user list — worth an explicit decision (§5.4) rather than silently assuming a cloud-only posture is fine everywhere in the country.
- **Messenger/WhatsApp/Viber, not just SMS/email**, are how PH clinics and patients already communicate. SMS-via-Twilio (our current plan, §6.9) works but costs per-message and isn't the channel patients actually use day to day.
- **GCash is already assumed as a payment method** by every PH competitor found (we already list it in §6.7 as a manual-entry payment method — good, we're aligned here, this is confirmation not a gap).
- **HMO friction is the single most automatable PH-specific pain point** — verifying HMO coverage and submitting an LOA is today a phone-call-and-paperwork process at most clinics; Agimat Health built its whole financial pitch around automating exactly this. Our current MVP only stores HMO claim info as notes (§6.7) — this is the highest-leverage PH-specific gap found in this research.

### 4.3 Already aligned with the market (🟢 — confirmed, no action needed)

- **AI ambient SOAP drafting from dictation/transcription** (§8.2) — matches the exact architecture pattern the entire AI-scribe market converged on (audio → transcript → LLM draft → human review). We are not behind here; if anything this is our strongest differentiator once shipped, since it's native to our own EHR rather than a bolt-on tool.
- **Draft-then-confirm AI writes, tiered risk model** (§9) — more rigorous than what most vendor marketing pages describe for their own AI features; worth highlighting in sales/marketing copy once live, not just treating as an internal safety detail.
- **Cloud-native from day one** — a real point in our favor vs. legacy desktop products retrofitted to the cloud (the Curve Dental vs. Dentrix Ascend distinction found in dental research applies to the general-EMR space too).
- **GCash as a payment method, SMS reminders, PH-configurable receipt numbering** — already scoped, consistent with what PH competitors treat as baseline.
- **Semantic chart search, no-show prediction, command palette** (§8.3, §8.8, §8.9) — modern touches most competitors, PH or international, don't have at all; genuine differentiators already in scope, just need to actually ship.

## 5. Open decisions — resolve with the user before any of §4 gets scheduled

1. **Patient self-service portal (§4.2 #2):** our own MVP explicitly deferred this in favor of the booking link, reasoning that the added auth-surface/consent complexity wasn't worth it for MVP. This research shows it's now closer to "expected" than "differentiator" internationally. Does this change the priority, or does the original MVP reasoning still hold for our specific segment (solo PH doctors, not US hospital systems)?
2. **Dental as a real vertical vs. "one of many specialties" (§4.2 #6):** building a real odontogram/perio-charting module is a meaningfully larger commitment than a SOAP template line. Is dental a genuine target segment worth that investment, or should we keep dentists served at "good enough" specialty-template level and not chase feature parity with Dentrix/Curve/Open Dental?
3. **PhilHealth/BIR/DOH compliance ownership (§3.2):** does kame-desk pursue its own accreditation/certification, partner with an existing accredited HITP/CAS-accredited vendor, or leave BIR/PhilHealth compliance entirely as "the clinic's own responsibility, we just provide configurable fields"? This is a business decision (cost, timeline, liability) that should happen before any engineering time goes into #1–#3 in §4.1.
4. **Offline resilience (§4.1 #5):** how far do we go — read-only cached patient lookup during a connectivity drop, or full offline write-and-sync (much harder, real conflict-resolution problems)? Needs its own scoping pass; don't let this silently become a big architecture change without sign-off, given `tech-stack.md` currently assumes an always-on backend.
5. **WhatsApp/Messenger/Viber (§4.1 #4):** which channel(s) actually matter for our first pilot clinics? Don't build all three speculatively — this should be validated with the actual pilot clinics referenced in `mvp.md` §15.7 rather than decided from vendor marketing pages alone.
6. **Scope and sequencing:** should any of this fold into the existing in-progress rebuild (`docs/workflow/in-progress/ground-up-app-redesign-and-platform-admin.md`, Phase 20–32) as new phases, or become its own separate tracked initiative once decisions above are made?
